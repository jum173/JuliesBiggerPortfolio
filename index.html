Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

All 3 of these things are involved in creating. Rhetoric is the conversation around the topic of what you're making in today's world and needs to be considered so that your creation isn't perceived as insensitive or lacking background information. Technology in creating plays a part in accessibility. Technology needs to be accessible to anyone of impairments or dissabilites. Ethics goes hand in hand with both of these things as the things you create must be of good moral standing, for example, creating an ad for a product that may make you money but is harmful to others needs to have all side effects and consequences of using on the advertisement for it to be ethical.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

I leveraged design principles by making sure my designs have colors that don't contrast or wash out my design. I also made sure the fonts I used made sense in regards to if it matched the vibe, if it was big enough and readable, and also changing the spacing and sizing of words to emphasize certain things and to organize things by section and header.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

I loved the workshops in my class with my peers. I think constructive critism is really helpful and it enables good revision. Also, being able to see my peers' designs and their perspective on things may have inspired my own designs and made me even more knowledgable on tools I could use within the design softwares.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

Revision is really important -- after the workshops, I was able to take into consideration the ideas my peers had about changes that should be made. They'd make comments about how spacing and coloring could be better, how my information may have been lengthy and needed shortening, etc. I was able to take these comments and go back into my work and make revisions, especially with the logos assignment.

5. Learn to present your work.

I had a lot of fun presenting in this class. I feel as though the class size was perfect enough to where it didn't feel so high-stakes and I was able to really hear my peers and what they had to say about my work. Presenting allows you to put your design intentions into words and also hear great feedback.

Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

All 3 of these things are involved in creating. Rhetoric is the conversation around the topic of what you're making in today's world and needs to be considered so that your creation isn't perceived as insensitive or lacking background information. Technology in creating plays a part in accessibility. Technology needs to be accessible to anyone of impairments or dissabilites. Ethics goes hand in hand with both of these things as the things you create must be of good moral standing, for example, creating an ad for a product that may make you money but is harmful to others needs to have all side effects and consequences of using on the advertisement for it to be ethical.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

Design principles I used were understanding what some colors mean and what emotions they evoke. I also utilized space and whiteness in keeping certain designs clean while others more jampacked to maximize fun and information, such as in the NJ infographic. In the magazine in particular, I learned a lot about how to leverage design principles; in making revisions to the alignment of text, in switching between whether I wanted the image or the title of the 2 page spread first, and deciding on which black and white filter to use on my images to give a clean, simple, but easy to see contrast. The magazine taught me how to make designs from page to page that compliment each other, yet aren't repetitive, yet aren't too similar.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

Working with others in a workshop was very helpful to my design progression. My peers were able to tell me how they interpretted by designs and I was able to decide if their interpretation matched my intention for the design. Additionally, seeing their designs and methodologies in creating through the softwares we were learning gave me my own inspiration and ideas for future designs. My goal for workshops and revisions were to ask my peers how they were doing certain things within Adobe and what tools they've figured out and liked. I wanted to learn new methods for designing. I also wanted to gather insight on my own designs through the perspectives of others... And I did!



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

In this class, I was able to practice revision. When my peers or professor give me feedback, or even when I spot something I can enhance or make better, I go back and revise my work. I do so with a trial-and-error approach where I duplicate designs and tweak things, seeing if they'll look better, keeping the safety of my original design in case it does not. My goals within revision were to get out of this mindset of "does revision or too many later-changes make it worse?" I used to draw a lot and a lot of times, I'd keep looking at my art and continue to add things, overral ruining what the previous finished product looked like. I needed to understand that design is differnt; you have undo buttons and also the ability to reverse anything you've done later on. Also, it's important to realize that if majority of people give you the same critique, you should probably do what they want versus what you want because in most cases, the point of design to is appeal to others.

5. Learn to present your work.

I think I was always good at presenting and showing my enthusiasm and curiosity. Presenting to my small group and also to the entire class made me even more comfortable with taking critism in real time and asking follow up questions for feedback.
